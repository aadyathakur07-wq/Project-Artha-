Learning Centre

Structured, jargon-free lessons designed for immediate everyday application.

Module A: Understanding Money & Needs vs. Wants

Financial literacy is the skill of using money intentionally. Needs are non-negotiable living expenses (rent, dal, rice, basic medicine). Wants enhance life but can be deferred (dining out, fashion upgrades). Reviewing expenses without self-judgement allows you to identify where money leaks occur.

Essential Foundation

Module B: Budgeting with Regular or Irregular Income

If your monthly income fluctuates (freelancing, gig work, tailoring, seasonal agricultural trading), budget using your lowest earning month from the past 6 months as your baseline. Any surplus in peak months must flow into an income-buffer account to protect low-income cycles.

Cash-Flow Planning

Module C: Emergency Funds & Safe Saving

An emergency fund is strictly for unexpected crises: sudden medical events, vehicle repair needed for livelihood, or temporary job loss. A realistic baseline target is 3 to 6 months of essential living costs kept in accessible, risk-free savings accounts or liquid recurring deposits.

Risk Protection

Module D: Demystifying Loans, EMIs & Borrowing Traps

Never evaluate a loan on monthly EMI alone. Lenders quoting a "flat interest rate" of 10% actually charge nearly 18% on a reducing-balance basis because you continue paying interest on money you already returned. Always demand the Total Cost of Borrowing including processing fees and GST.

Debt Management

Module E: Digital Payment Safety & Fraud Prevention

Crucial rule of UPI: You NEVER need to enter your UPI PIN, scan a QR code, or approve a collect request to RECEIVE money. Entering your PIN always debits money from your account. If defrauded, dial 1930 immediately or log on to cybercrime.gov.in within 2 hours to freeze illicit transfers.

About Project Artha — Her Economy

Democratising practical financial education to advance inclusive economic growth.

Our Mission

Project Artha — Her Economy aims to make practical financial knowledge more accessible through approachable educational resources and tools. By helping women and young adults understand budgeting, savings, borrowing, and digital financial safety, the project seeks to support more informed everyday financial decisions.

Alignment with UN SDG 8

Sustainable Development Goal 8 promotes sustained, inclusive economic growth and decent work for all. Expanding practical financial awareness ensures individuals maintain control over their earned income and avoid predatory debt.

Why Kannada-First?

Critical financial decisions are best understood in one's primary language. Providing high-fidelity Kannada financial materials breaks barriers created by English-centric financial jargon.
